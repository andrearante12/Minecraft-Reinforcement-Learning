Milestone 2 Results

PPO vs Dreamer on the vision hunting task, 50,000 game steps each, same inputs, same 12 actions, same reward.

Result in one line

Neither agent learned to hunt within 50,000 steps: PPO made 2 kills and Dreamer made 3, out of about 69 training episodes each. At this budget the sample-efficiency comparison is inconclusive. The training logs do show why, and point to what to change.

What the logs say

  • PPO learned to lose less, not to hunt. Mean reward rose from −56 to −25 across the run, mostly by dying less early: more timeouts and fewer pit deaths in the last third. Its action entropy fell from 2.44 to 2.26, so the policy did narrow.
  • Dreamer’s world model learned to see but not to score. Its prediction error on the DINOv2 features fell from 1.54 to 1.16 and on proprio from 2.67 to 0.21. Its reward-prediction loss stayed flat (0.10 to 0.11). With only 3 kills in its replay buffer there was almost nothing to learn a kill reward from.
  • So Dreamer’s actor had nothing to aim for. Imagined returns stayed negative (−4.8 to −4.4), and the actor stayed close to random: entropy 2.36, where 2.48 is uniform over 12 actions.
  • Kills are the bottleneck for both. A kill takes 3 hits on a fleeing 10-HP animal. A random-ish policy almost never gets there, so neither method sees enough success to learn from in 50k steps.

Suggested next steps

  • More steps for both. PPO is already continuing from its 50k checkpoint (1M-step budget). Dreamer needs the same before the comparison means anything.
  • Start on the easier rung. Level 0 (flat walled arena, 1 cow, 200-step episodes) gives far more encounters per step. A curriculum L0 → L1 would get both agents their first kills sooner.
  • Cut the sinkhole rate while learning to hunt. Desert loses about 80% of episodes to the sinkhole. Turning hazards on after the first kills keeps the hazard study without starving the hunting signal.
  • Report the planner on a model that predicts reward. The lookahead planner scores futures by predicted reward, which this model can’t yet predict. Evaluate it on a checkpoint whose reward loss has actually dropped.
  • Use more than one seed before claiming a winner. Each agent ran once, and at 2 vs 3 kills the difference is noise.

Bug found and fixed: false pits on slopes

The first forest evaluation ended every episode “stuck in a pit” at exactly step 25. The pit check counted the agent as trapped whenever ground 2–3 blocks away was 2+ blocks higher than its feet, which is true on any hillside. In the hilly forest, 52% of standable ground, including the spawn point, counted as a pit. In the training biomes it was 1–10% (tundra 1%, meadow 3%, plains 8%, desert 10%), so some training deaths were really the agent standing on a slope. That affected both agents equally.

The new check asks whether the agent can walk out: it searches outward over steps that rise at most one block, and the agent is only trapped if it cannot get 5 blocks away. Now 0 open-ground columns count as a pit in the training biomes (5 natural hollows in forest), and every sinkhole and carved-pit floor still does. The forest results from before the fix are void and are being re-run.
